# Frequently Asked Questions About AI in Qualitative Research

## Can AI Replace Human Coding?

No. AI cannot replace human coding in qualitative research, and anyone claiming otherwise is selling something. What AI can do is handle mechanical parts: pattern recognition across large datasets, initial category suggestions, and rapid retrieval of relevant segments. But coding is not just labeling. It involves interpretive judgment, contextual awareness, and theoretical sensitivity. A machine cannot know why a participant paused before answering, what cultural subtext sits beneath a phrase, or how a single outlier quote might reshape your entire thematic structure.

The useful question is whether AI can make human coding faster and more systematic. The answer is yes, provided the tool maintains a clear chain of evidence from raw data to final code. That evidence linking is what separates useful AI assistance from a black box that produces output you cannot verify.

## How Does Evidence Linking Prevent Hallucinations?

Evidence linking attaches every AI-generated code, theme, or summary directly to the specific data segments that produced it. When QInsights surfaces a theme, you can click through to the exact participant quote or passage that the AI used as evidence. This creates a traceable path from interpretation back to source material.

Hallucinations happen when a language model generates plausible-sounding content with no grounding in your actual data. Evidence linking prevents this in two ways. First, it constrains the AI to work within your dataset only. The model cannot invent external examples or fabricate quotes because every output must reference a real segment. Second, it gives you a direct audit trail. If a theme looks suspicious, you inspect the evidence. If the evidence is weak or missing, you discard the theme. No blind trust required.

This is not a guarantee against all errors. A model can still misclassify a quote or suggest a theme that does not hold up under scrutiny. But evidence linking turns those errors from invisible failures into visible, correctable ones.

## Is GDPR Compliance Possible With Cloud-Based Tools?

Yes, but not automatically. GDPR compliance depends on three factors: data residency, processing agreements, and anonymization controls.

Data residency means your raw transcripts and recordings must remain within a jurisdiction that meets GDPR requirements. Some cloud tools store everything on US servers subject to the Cloud Act, which can conflict with European data protection law. Others offer EU-based hosting or allow you to choose your region. You need to verify this before uploading anything.

Processing agreements are the legal contracts between you and the tool provider. A GDPR-compliant tool will sign a Data Processing Agreement (DPA) that defines what they can and cannot do with your data. No DPA means no compliance, regardless of technical safeguards.

Anonymization controls matter because qualitative data is often highly identifiable. A single story about a rare medical condition or a specific workplace incident can identify a participant even without their name. The tool should offer ways to redact or pseudonymize at the import stage, before the AI processes anything.

QInsights provides options for local data processing and encrypted storage. But you should always confirm the specific compliance posture of any tool against your institution's or client's requirements. A general privacy policy is not enough.

## How Transparent Are AI-Generated Codebooks?

Transparency varies enormously across tools. Some produce a codebook as a static PDF with no way to see how each code was derived. Others generate codebooks where each code entry includes its definition, inclusion criteria, and links to every data segment that informed it.

The most transparent codebooks also expose the AI's confidence level for each code assignment. A 95% confidence assignment that a passage belongs to a theme should be treated differently than a 60% confidence assignment that needs human review.

If your tool cannot show you exactly why a code was applied to a specific quote, the codebook is not transparent enough for rigorous qualitative research. You should be able to trace every code back to a concrete decision rule or pattern the AI identified in the data. Otherwise, you are publishing claims you cannot defend.

## What Are the Limits of Current AI Tools?

Current tools struggle with sarcasm, irony, and non-literal language. They can miss subtle shifts in tone across a single interview. They sometimes over-aggregate, collapsing distinct but related concepts into one theme because the surface-level language looks similar. And they require clean, well-formatted data. A transcript with heavy overlapping speech, accents, or poor transcription quality will produce unreliable results.

These limits are not reasons to avoid AI. They are reasons to use it as a research assistant rather than a research replacement. The best workflows involve AI for initial coding and pattern detection, then human review for refinement, contradiction checking, and theoretical integration.
