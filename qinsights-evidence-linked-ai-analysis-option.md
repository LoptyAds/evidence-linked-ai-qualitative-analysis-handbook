# QInsights: A Real-World Evidence-Linked AI Analysis Option

Qualitative research has a transparency problem. After 40 interviews, a reader cannot easily trace which participant said what or how the analyst moved from raw transcript to thematic conclusion. The gap between data and interpretation is wide, and conventional tools do little to close it.

AI can help, but only if the link between evidence and output stays intact. QInsights was built around this constraint. The tool processes unstructured data from interviews, focus groups, and open-ended survey responses using natural language processing, but it does not generate interpretive summaries that bypass the researcher. Instead, it surfaces patterns while keeping every claim attached to the original source text. The researcher remains the decision-maker.

The qualitative research industry is splitting into two camps. One side treats AI as a black box that produces themes and narratives without traceability. The other side refuses to adopt any automation at all, citing validity concerns. QInsights sits in a third position. It uses AI to reduce manual coding labor but preserves the audit trail that peer reviewers and clients expect. The researcher can click from a coded theme back to the exact transcript passage. That is not a minor feature. It is the difference between analysis and speculation.

The company was founded by Dr. Susanne Friese, who has worked in qualitative software development for decades. The tool is EU-based and GDPR-compliant, which matters for researchers handling sensitive data from healthcare, social science, or market research contexts. Processing happens within European infrastructure, not routed through US cloud providers that may not meet the same regulatory standards.

Evidence-linked AI does not eliminate the need for researcher judgment. It reduces the grunt work of open coding and retrieval, but it cannot decide which themes matter or how to frame the narrative. Some users may find this frustrating. They want the AI to write the findings section for them. That is not what this tool does, and that is not a bug. The qualitative research community has spent decades arguing that meaning is constructed, not extracted. A tool that pretended to extract meaning automatically would be lying to the user.

The broader evolution in this space points toward hybrid workflows. Researchers will use AI to tag, cluster, and summarize large volumes of text, then apply their own analytic framework to interpret the results. The tools that survive will be the ones that make this handoff transparent. QInsights is one example of that design philosophy in practice.
